The letter

An open letter to the people writing open letters

To everyone demanding the NVIDIA Board bring him back,

We are people who read past the headline. We write with gratitude for your enthusiasm and with one clear demand: stop.

Your letter asks for due process. Good. So do we. Due process is an investigation followed by a decision. Reinstating someone first and establishing the facts second is the same two steps in the wrong order.

Your letter says he was removed. The company's filing says he resigned. Your letter says you have not been shown the evidence. Correct. Neither have we. That is what "the investigation has not concluded" means. It does not mean "so put him back."

Your letter calls this mob justice. A board of directors asking questions of a chief executive is not a mob. It is the one thing a board is for. The mob, if we are counting, is the six-figure crowd with the hashtag.

Some of the loudest names on your letter run companies that buy these chips by the truckload. We are sure that is a coincidence.

We ask you to take three actions:

1

Read his own email. He told employees he was unfaithful and that there was no excuse. If he isn't arguing with that, you don't need to argue it for him.

2

Wait for the investigation you asked for. The board has one. Congress has several. He has agreed to cooperate with them. You could too, by doing nothing.

3

Find a second idea. A company worth this much can survive a job search. If it can't, that was the actual governance problem, and it is thirty-three years old.

Thirty-three years of leadership do not place anyone above accountability. That sentence is from your letter. It was a good sentence. You could have stopped there.

Thank you for your service. Please log off.

Signed by the people listed below, each speaking for themselves and each fully capable of reading a press release.
